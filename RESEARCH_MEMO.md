### 0. The question, and the symmetry check

The owner's prior, stated before this study: "my fills tend to land at the worst price within ±3 trading days." It is a negative self-assessment, so the three guards the vault applies to any favourable backtest claim apply here, inline, before anything is computed.

- **Source.** The claim is a feeling with salient examples attached; nothing in the vault tests it (section 5). The ledger holds 159 fills in 2026, of which 151 can be placed against a finalised seven-session window, so the claim is testable at that n against a simulated zero-skill null rather than against a remembered sample of bad fills.
- **Attribution.** Four structural mechanisms could produce the appearance without any deficit in the owner's timing, and each is tested rather than assumed away: a one-session dating offset between the ledger and the feed (section 2.1); an ex-dividend date inside the window (2.2); mechanical short-horizon reversal in the names traded (2.3); and the base rate, since the middle session of a seven-session window is rarely its extreme, so the naive one-in-seven for "worst of seven" is wrong and the null measures what the true rate is.
- **Anchor.** The comparator is the same trade, same side, same name, on a random session within sixty sessions of the fill, at a random price inside that session's range: zero skill at the day level and at the intraday level. The feed carries no volume, so the session's volume-weighted price is not available and the uniform draw is the intraday benchmark. The floor that would confirm the prior is fixed in advance and translated into basis points so that "real" and "material" are kept apart (section 3).

**1.3 Window and primary metric.** Sessions t−3 … t+3 on the ticker's own bar calendar, seven bars. All seven are rebased to the fill session's adjustment factor (`price × f_bar / f_t`), so prices on both sides of an ex-date are on one basis; a bar on the fill session itself is unchanged. H is the highest rebased high and L the lowest rebased low of the seven. Adverse rank u = (p − L)/(H − L) for a buy and (H − p)/(H − L) for a sell: 1 is the worst price available in the window for that side, 0 the best. H1, the single verdict-bearing clause: the equal-weighted mean of u across the 151 fills against the placebo distribution of the same mean.

**1.4 Secondaries, all declared, none verdict-bearing.** H2 (the literal reading, a floored secondary): the share of fills with u above 0.9, against the placebo share; a pass at its floor adds the suffix TAIL-ELEVATED and never confirms on its own. S1: mean u weighted by SGD notional at fill-date FX. S2: the share of fills worse than all six neighbouring closes (the fill session's own close excluded). S3: pre leg, side × (p / c_{t−3} − 1), positive when the owner bought after a rise or sold after a fall ("chased"); post leg, −side × (c_{t+3} / p − 1), positive when the price fell after a buy or rose after a sell ("it reversed"); both in per cent, each against its placebo. S4: the post leg against a placebo post leg matched on the pre leg, so that reversal that is mechanical in the name is netted out: for each fill the 10,000 placebos are cut into terciles on their own pre leg and the matched comparator is the placebo post leg in the tercile the actual fill's pre leg falls in; the matched null draws independently per fill, so S4 is a reversal check and not the verdict comparator. S5: intraday placement v, the adverse rank of the fill price inside its own session's range, against the uniform mean 0.5 with standard error 1/√(12 n), reported for all fills and by side; the standard error treats the fills as independent, so S5 is a descriptive read; this separates "wrong time of day" from "wrong day". S6: u computed at the session close in place of the fill price, against its placebo, the "wrong day" component alone. S7: buys and sells separately, for u, both legs and the tail share. S8: H1 with the six ex-date windows excluded, the sensitivity to the rebasing choice. S9: thinness, the H1 effect with each ticker's fills dropped in turn; a sign change on any single drop adds the suffix (THIN).

**1.5 Null.** The 151 fills are not independent observations: they sit on 68 session dates, 127 of them share a date with at least one other fill, and fills on one name whose seven-session windows overlap share bars. Under the null the u values of such fills move together with the market move they share, so the null must keep that structure. Fills that share a session date, or share a name with window centres within six bars, form one cluster (the union of both relations): 47 clusters, of which 17 are single fills, 14 pairs, 5 triples, 4 of four, 2 of five, 2 of six, one of seven, and two long chains of 19 and 27 fills linked through shared dates and overlapping windows. In each placebo set every member of a cluster takes the same offset, drawn uniformly from the offsets 4 to 60 bars before or after the fill session that are eligible for every member (a complete clean seven-bar window; no cluster lacked a common offset), and its own independent uniform price inside the placebo session's unadjusted low-to-high range; single fills draw their own offset. Every fill has at least 57 eligible sessions (median 114); seven fills draw from one side only (NASA 2026-04-06 and SPCX 2026-06-18, listed in 2026, draw after the fill; the five fills of 2026-09-25 draw before it). Each placebo is scored through the same vectorised function as the actual fill, same side, same rebasing to its own centre bar, and the run asserts parity between the vectorised and the scalar path on every actual fill. 10,000 placebo sets (the k-th draw of every fill), `numpy.random.default_rng(20261003)`; the one-sided p is the share of set means at or above the actual with the +1 correction, and the "better" direction is reported beside it; the 95 per cent interval on each actual mean is a 2,000-draw bootstrap over the same clusters (seed 20261004). Read at the freeze: mean u 0.4982, sd of the set mean 0.0212 (0.0191 under an independent-per-fill null at 1,000 draws, the structure first registered and dropped at the red-team's finding, section 1.12); tail share 3.99 per cent (sd 0.0165); 9.2 per cent of placebo fills worse than all six neighbouring closes; the pre leg +0.15 per cent and the post leg −0.31 per cent (drift in these names over 2026); 3.3 per cent of placebo windows hold an ex-date. The null is simulated rather than assumed because the middle session of a seven-session window is rarely its extreme: a placebo fill lands within a tenth of the worst price (u above 0.9) 4.0 per cent of the time and beyond all six neighbouring closes 9.2 per cent of the time, against the naive one in seven (14.3 per cent) for "the worst of seven sessions".

**1.6 Decision rule, floors, power.** One-sided α = 0.05 on H1. Floor δ = 0.06 in mean u: the minimum detectable effect at 0.80 power under the blocked null is 0.0526, and δ is that rounded up to the next 0.01; at the median window width of 13.7 per cent of price (p25 8.4, p75 20.3) it is 82 bps of price per fill, about 3.7 times one-way commission, a size at which the prior would be a material execution cost rather than a nuisance. Power at δ from the null sd, Φ(0.06 / 0.0212 − 1.645) = 0.883. The economic floor first registered, 0.05 (68 bps, about three times one-way commission), has power 0.764 under the blocked null (0.834 under the independent null) and is reported as a disclosure, never verdict-bearing. Power at 0.02 (a commission-sized effect, 27 bps) is 0.242, so a REJECTED verdict rules out an effect of 0.06 or more and leaves effects below about 0.053 open; reaching 0.80 power at 0.05 needs about 168 fills, at 0.02 about 1,050 fills (about 900 more, four and a half years at the 2026 rate of roughly 200 complete fills a year), and 0.90 power at δ about 161, all approximate, scaling the null sd by 1/√n and assuming the clustering ratio holds as the ledger grows. H2 floor: the minimum detectable effect at 0.80 power under the blocked null, 0.0409, rounded up to 0.05 (five percentage points of fills, eight of 151), power 0.918. Both floors are the null's own minimum detectable effect rounded up, so on both clauses power of at least 0.80 is a construction and not a finding, and the demotion rule (a clause below 0.80 at its floor is demoted, a demoted fail reads UNRESOLVED and a demoted pass SUGGESTIVE) cannot fire on either; it is carried for the record, and this is said here so that "powered" is not read as a test the clauses could have failed. What the floors do decide is the size the study can speak to: 0.06 in u, 82 bps at the median window, and the disclosure at 0.05 (read from H1's effect against 0.05; no separate field is written) tells the reader whether the first-registered economic floor would have changed the reading.

**1.7 Verdict mapping.** CONFIRMED: H1 p ≤ 0.05 and effect ≥ 0.06. DETECTED-BELOW-FLOOR: p ≤ 0.05 and effect below 0.06 (a real but sub-floor adverse placement). REJECTED: p > 0.05 at power 0.88 (no adverse placement of 0.06 or more at this n; the opposite direction is reported if its p ≤ 0.05). SUGGESTIVE and UNRESOLVED only if the power rule binds, which it does not. Suffix TAIL-ELEVATED when H2 passes at its floor; suffix (THIN) when a passing H1's effect changes sign on dropping any one ticker (thinness guards a pass; a REJECTED verdict carries no thinness suffix). The S$ anchor (section 3) is reported whatever the verdict and is the sizing statement, not a gate.

CONFIRMED or DETECTED-BELOW-FLOOR: the prior stands as evidenced at the stated size; any change to how orders are placed (limit at the prior close, staged entries, avoiding the first hour) is a separate owner decision, logged in its own record, and is not implied by this result. ### 2. The three ways this could be silently wrong, and the check in code

**2.1 Date alignment.** A US fill placed in Singapore evening hours could have been dated by its SGT date, one calendar day after the US session; a one-session offset would manufacture "worst-price" fills by itself. Checked before the freeze on every fill with a feed: 152 of 154 sit inside their dated session's range (98.7 per cent), and 90 of those 152 sit outside the previous session's range, so a one-session-late dating would have put them outside their day and did not. Zero fills were re-dated. The two outside their day are the 2525.HK pair whose feed is in other units (section 1.2), not a dating question. The convention is established: `trades.json` dates a fill by its exchange session. The re-dating rule (prefer the previous session, else the next, else exclude) was registered and never needed.

**2.2 Adjusted against unadjusted prices; ex-dates and splits inside the window.** The metric uses unadjusted bars; `ac` would shift every pre-ex bar and make a pre-ex buy look like buying the top (the ES3 ex-dividend incident of 2026-08-13 is the vault's record of exactly this). Ex-dates are detected as a change in `f = ac / c` between consecutive finalised bars beyond 2 × 10⁻⁴; splits are already applied by the feed to `o/h/l/c` and would appear as a reverted jump if they were not. Six fills carry an ex-date inside their window: MAR.US 2026-03-02 S (0.19 per cent), OXY.US 2026-03-10 S (0.47), NVDA.US 2026-06-01 B and 2026-06-05 S (0.12), DELL.US 2026-07-22 B (0.17) and 2338.HK 2026-09-25 S (2.00). The primary rebases the window to the fill session's factor; S8 excludes the six. S8 is one-sided by design: the six actual fills are dropped while the remaining fills' placebo windows keep their rebased ex-dates (3.3 per cent of placebo windows hold one, all small), an asymmetry immaterial at the distribution sizes found and stated rather than engineered away. A dividend the feed has not published cannot be detected by either route and is a stated limit.

**2.3 Mechanical reversal.** Short-horizon reversal exists in many markets, so buying after a three-session rally can look like bad timing without being a timing deficit. S3 splits the window into "I chased" (pre leg) and "it reversed after me" (post leg); S4 compares the post leg with a placebo post leg drawn from placebo sessions whose own pre leg sits in the same tercile as the fill's, so that the reversal a random trader would have met after the same kind of run is netted out. Only the excess of the actual post leg over the matched placebo is read as reversal following the owner's trades.

### 3. The S$ anchor (Step 3, design)

For each fill the placebo-median price is the price inside the fill's own seven-session range at the median placebo u: P_ref = L + u_med (H − L) for a buy and H − u_med (H − L) for a sell, so in both cases cost = (u − u_med) (H − L) × quantity × fill-date FX to SGD. Summed over the 151 fills; the null is the same sum on each of the 10,000 placebo sets, which gives the p and the sd in S$. Expressed in bps of the SGD traded notional (S$1,662,184 across the 151 fills: buys S$923,673, sells S$738,511) and as a share of 2026 P&L. Inputs with their provenance: the 2026 P&L is the dashboard's YTD P&L card, +S$194,578 (+20.72 per cent), read from the live page on 2026-10-03 at about 14:02 SGT with 55 live quotes and the intraday card baked to 3 October; it is the output of the dashboard's FX-aware total-return FIFO engine and is single-source, so the share is an approximate ratio; the figure is pinned in `prereg_spec.json` rather than passed at run time. Commission: measured from the ledger's own `fee` field, carried on 149 of the 151 fills, as Σ fee / Σ (quantity × price) by currency and, pooled, in SGD terms with every term converted at fill-date FX (a raw pooled ratio would mix currencies); the 2026-08-02 rate-card record (21.8 bps one-way USD/EUR/JPY/AUD, 33.7 bps HKD, 41.2 bps SGD on one fill; register id `2026-08-02-Portfolio-Command-Centre-2`) is quoted beside it as an unreviewed extraction. The reading rule, fixed now: the effect is "material" if its bps of SGD notional exceed one-way commission in SGD terms, "real but sub-commission" if H1 passes with bps below it, and "absent at the registered size" on REJECTED, with the S$ point estimate and its null sd reported in every case.

### 4. Sell regret (Step 4, exploratory, after the verdict)

For each of the 67 complete sells, the SGD total return of the name sold from the fill price to the close 20 and 60 sessions later (`ac` ratio for the dividend, fill-date and end-date FX), against what the proceeds went into: same-currency buys dated within seven calendar days after the sell, weighted by SGD notional, else foreign cash earning the FX move. Regret = return of the name sold − return of the destination, positive when selling was regrettable on that horizon. Data allow 62 sells at 20 sessions and 49 at 60. Carried caveats: 4004.T is excluded (the Crasus spin-off is unbooked and the mark is distorted); `ref` exists on 10 of the 151 fills, all from 2026-08-20, so fills cannot be grouped by decision type; the destination rule is a tracing heuristic, not the cash ledger. Nothing here is verdict-bearing.

**What the decomposition says.** The adverse placement is the day, not the time of day. Over the three sessions before the fill the price moved 5.2 per cent against the owner on average (placebo +0.2 per cent): buys followed three-session rises of 6.5 per cent (placebo +0.9), sells followed falls of 3.5 per cent (placebo: a 0.75 per cent rise), and 82 per cent of fills on each side had an adverse pre leg (median +3.8 per cent). The fill priced at the session close gives the same u (0.645), so the choice of day carries the whole effect. After the fill, buys gave back 1.2 per cent over three sessions while the placebo rose 1.15 per cent (+2.4pp adverse, p 0.01), and that exceeds what a random trader meeting a top-tercile pre move would have seen by 1.2pp (p 0.02); sells did not reverse (price +0.6 per cent after sells against +0.7 placebo, p 0.53). The pre-matching is by tercile, so with actual pre moves far inside the top tercile the reversal excess is an upper bound on what mechanical reversal leaves unexplained. The intraday placement is adverse too (0.575 of the session's range, 27 per cent of fills in the worst quarter of their day) but adds nothing to u beyond the day choice: the fill sits marginally better than the close in the window (0.639 against 0.645), consistent with trading in the direction of the session's move.

Attribution: the three structural explanations are excluded and the base rate is handled; what remains is behaviour, the entry and exit trigger reacting to a sharp three-session move, which is the same signature the vault found for mechanical close-cross breakout entries on the S&P 900 (`2026-08-06-stock-radar-1`, a robust anti-edge) and for all-time-high entries (`2026-09-03-stock-radar-1`); the intraday placement is a smaller, separate component.
